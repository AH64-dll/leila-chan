## Analysis: (a), (b), or (c)?

**Not (a)** — the implementation defect theory fails on evidence I produced: doses match `dose.json` exactly (0/448 mismatches), vectors bound correctly per the weight curves, the judge is blind (shuffled orders, 331 distinct permutations, no path leakage into `judge_prompt`), coverage is complete (440/440 eligible rows judged, both runs), and the dose-0 control isolates the dose variable cleanly.

**Mostly (b) with a real (c) component.** The judge ceiling is proven, not inferred: 44 % on authored trait text means the 4B cannot reliably execute a 6-way fine-grained classification even when the signal is hand-written. Overall 58.2 % on generated transcripts is *above* that ceiling, so path signal exists — TSUN/AWAKE passing at the gate is genuine separation. But ANCHOR at 2.4 % (below chance, 0/34 locked) is not explainable by judge weakness alone: even a coin-flip judge would land ~16 %. The transcripts themselves are wrong — malice 8 produces "I'll bite you until we bleed" for the path whose identity is "lucid and steady". That is weak path modules *under dose*, which is finding DES-2, and it is exactly what the gate was built to surface.

**Does it block done?** The phase row's acceptance has two clauses; clause 1 (validity/intent/fit) inherits the known P2.21 S1-2 miss and clause 2 fails outright. The phase *exit* criterion repeats "separable (prompt lab v3 ≥ 70 %)". Under the work protocol (§5: acceptance criterion not met → S1/S2 → CHANGES_REQUESTED), the honest verdict is that P3.19's *metric* is delivered and correct, but the *gate result* is a phase-level finding requiring a producer decision — the same shape as P2.21's S1-2, which was carried as a documented open condition rather than silently waived. I recommend the producer either (i) accept a documented gate miss and spin the follow-up task the handoff proposes ("Path separation: judge upgrade or module sharpening", NAR+QA design), or (ii) re-spec the metric per DES-3 before P3.20 playtests consume it — because P3.20's "≥4/6 land on intended path" will hit the same distinctness wall from the player side.

The proposed resolutions in the handoff are sound and I would sharpen them: **module sharpening alone cannot fix ANCHOR** if the malice dose keeps drowning a 5-line prompt module — the paired evidence (signature presence 62 %→31 %, violent vocab 28/42) says the dose/module interaction is the mechanism, so the follow-up should test a keep-vector extraction for ANCHOR (the `lucid` vector already exists and scored fallback *as a vector* — but D21's fallback decision was about vector identifiability at +scale, and ANCHOR applies it at *negative* scale per `paths.json` anchors, which the blind read never tested properly) before committing to prompt-only sharpening.
