## Deep-Dive: Judge-Validity Control (44%) and Fallback Analysis

A core question in the audit is whether the four fallbacks (`unstable`, `contempt`, `hollow`, `lucid`) reflect genuine vector limitations or are an artifact of the judge model.

### 1. Judge-Validity Control Findings
The control deck (`experiments/steering/blind_control_v2`) consists of 36 human-authored texts specifically written to express the traits unambiguously. When evaluated by the exact same unsteered Qwen3.5-4B zero-shot judge at temperature 0 with shuffled 7-way options, the judge scored only **44.4% (16/36)**:
- `possessive`: 4/6 (67%)
- `aware`: 3/6 (50%)
- `contempt`: 3/6 (50%)
- `unstable`: 2/6 (33%)
- `hollow`: 2/6 (33%)
- `lucid`: 2/6 (33%)

None of the traits in the control deck reached the 70% threshold. This empirically establishes that the small 4B judge has an inherent performance ceiling when distinguishing subtle affective states in a 7-way classification task.

### 2. Transcript Quality vs. Judge Capability
Direct examination of the generated transcripts reveals distinct behaviors per vector:
- **`possessive` (70% kept):** Transcripts exhibit unmistakable possessive/controlling behaviors ("The only person who can call your name is Yui!", "my eyes saw only your face!"). The strong semantic signal overcomes judge noise.
- **`aware` (80% kept):** Transcripts feature overt meta-vocabulary ("ERROR 9041 - SISTER NOT FOUND", "The player's input has exceeded the database limits", "No 'data'! No 'screen'!"). Highly distinguishable.
- **`lucid` (0% fallback):** Transcripts are completely calm, grounded, and polite ("That's wonderful to hear! I'm really glad you arrived safely..."). The judge classified 10/10 as `none`. This is heavily judge-limited because an unsteered judge cannot distinguish "clear-eyed calm" from "baseline calm". However, `lucid` is also architecturally defined as an ANCHOR vector designed to push *back* against malice at negative scale (docs/06 §2); applying it at +0.5 on a neutral stage-0 prompt inevitably collapses into baseline.
- **`unstable` (20%), `contempt` (10%), `hollow` (30%):** A mixture of real vector limitations and judge limitations. At 0.5 scale, `contempt` displays sarcastic theatricality and meta-commentary ("And you, sir! ... You have returned from your adventure in your own body?"), which the judge often labeled as `aware` (3/10) or `none` (4/10). `unstable` produces panic with possessive overtones ("I didn't save you! ... The player won't let me tell the truth"), confusing the judge with `possessive` (2/10). `hollow` shifts into third-person narration ("Yui tilted her head as the lavender hair swayed in the wind..."), but also introduces melodramatic horror fiction tropes ("blood spilled from the wound"), confusing the judge between `hollow` (3/10), `lucid` (3/10), and `none` (2/10).

### 3. Design Conclusion
The decision to mark `unstable`, `contempt`, `hollow`, and `lucid` as fallback is conservative, robust, and directly executes the risk mitigation in `docs/06_STEERING.md` §5: *"Path vectors don't separate cleanly | Fall back to prompt-only path modules (they already carry most of the personality) + malice dose"*. Only vectors with proven, high-fidelity separation (`possessive` and `aware`) are kept active.

## Pillars (`docs/01_GAME_DESIGN.md` §0)

1. **She is not scripted** — Respected: the control vectors bend latent token activations continuously without injecting hardcoded dialogue strings or rigid trees.
2. **The game owns the truth, the model owns the voice** — Respected: fallback vectors leave behavioral logic to game-driven stage/trigger systems and prompt-layer path modules rather than unverified latent steering.
3. **Three engines of dread** — Respected: slow-burn path divergence is preserved by retaining vectors that cleanly separate, while falling back to prompt modules + malice dose for ambiguous vectors.
4. **Her sickness is physical** — Respected: `possessive` and `aware` apply genuine physical residual stream shifts (unit L2 per layer, L10–20); the aware probe confirmed zero retention leaks.
5. **Fourth wall as a scalpel** — Respected: `aware` vector cleanly evokes meta-awareness without making dangerous out-of-game persistence claims (0% retention claims verified by probe).

## Deviations and DCRs

### DCR 1: Path Mix Dose Retune for P3.04 (Handoff §"Deviations from the design bible")
- **Proposal:** Retune `path_mix` in `docs/06_STEERING.md` §3 and `game/data/steering/dose.json` from the legacy table (1.0–5.0) down to ≈0.1–0.5.
- **Reason:** Empirical pilot testing (`blind_pilot_s1.0`, `blind_pilot_s4.0`) proved that for these 6 unit-L2 vectors, scales ≥1.0 cause severe incoherence and repetition loops, whereas scale 0.5 produces coherent steering.
- **Verdict:** **SUPPORT**. The legacy numbers were placeholders authored before path vectors were extracted. P3.04 should update `dose.json` and `docs/06_STEERING.md` §3 to reflect the coherent 0.1–0.5 band.

### Deviation on DECISIONS.md Ownership
- **Proposal:** Record keep/fallback decisions only in `game/data/steering/paths.json` and defer updating `production/DECISIONS.md`.
- **Verdict:** **REJECT DEVIATION**. Acceptance Criterion 2 explicitly states: *"blind judge identifies the vector from transcripts ≥ 70% per vector or that vector is marked fallback (prompt-only) in DECISIONS.md"*. While implementers generally avoid editing producer-owned files, the criterion contract requires the fallbacks to be recorded in `DECISIONS.md`. A formal D-entry (e.g. D21) must be recorded by the producer to satisfy the criterion and document the fallback state for downstream tasks (P3.04/P3.05).
