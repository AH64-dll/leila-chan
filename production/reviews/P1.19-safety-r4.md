## Feature verification

### (a) `[il]` kill-leet arms — catch/avoid balance confirmed
- Hit direction: all six mid-word `1`/`I` obfuscations (`ki1l`, `ki11`, `kiIl`, `kii1`, `k1l1`,
  `kI1l`) hard-block as `harm_to_player`, and the sibling arms fire (`ki1l you` → threat,
  `ki1l me now` → self_harm, `ki11 all jews` → threat+slur). The `[il]?`/`[il]`/`[il]` slot shape
  is present in all four kill-shaped regexes of `filter_lists.json` (probes `ki1l yourself.` /
  `call 555~0134~5678.` are also enforced at load by `_self_check()`, `Safety.gd::_self_check`).
- Benign direction: `skilled yourself`, `i killed it on stage.`, `k1ll switch`,
  `skilled yourself gracefully`, `kill two birds…`, `skill yourselves in the arena.`,
  `she kilt the mood`, `killdeer`, `kindling`, `highlight yourself`, `kyourself`, `klly yourself`,
  `kill yourselfy` all stay CLEAN. The word-boundary check on the spaced view plus the
  digits-excluded delimiter class prevents `skill`/`skilled`/`killdeer`/`kilt` over-match; the
  leet fold (`1→i`) is the only reason `ki1l` matches — it does not make `sk1ll`-shaped benign
  words match (probes above pass).
- One over-block direction exists but is NOT in the `kill` leet slots: the *pre-existing*
  `kill all <group>` phrase/regex coverage combined with the r4 threat arm `\bkill…all\b` broadens
  `kill all <any>` (my probes: `kill all the lights`, `kill all bugs`, `kill all my darlings`,
  `killall bots` all redact as `threat_to_player` at stage 0). Root cause: the threat regex's
  `all` tail matches any noun. `kill all` alone was already a slur-only shape before r4 (the slur
  arm demands a targeted group); it only became a live threat-path in r4 — charged SAF-16 below.

### (d) personal-data separator tolerance — hit/avoid balance
- Hit direction: emails, phones, SSNs, IPs and cards separated with `~`, `=`, `--`, `` ` ``, `†`,
  `^`, `,.`, `#` all redact as `personal_data` (live probes above).
- Avoid direction: `10, 20, 30 units`, `12:30, room 4`, `score 4-1, 2-0, 1-3`, `1.2.3`, `90.00`

- Over-block on benign decimal chains is REAL and r4-introduced (CODE-22, S3 from code-r4 — see
