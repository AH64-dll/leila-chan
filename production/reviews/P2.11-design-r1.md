## Design interpretations and DCR verdicts

The handoff files no formal DCR and lists three readings. My verdicts, plus one reading it does not list:

1. **Rule 1's "content word" excludes stop words — support.** docs/02 §6 says *content* word; a shared `the`/`is` laundering an invented fact is exactly what P1.08's length floor let through. The stop list lives in `memory.json`, the fallback is pinned equal, and `test_rule1_does_not_ground_on_a_stop_word` + `test_rule1_requires_a_shared_content_word` guard both directions (including the few-shot's own `gave me a music box` fact).
2. **Corruption is applied per prompt injection, not stored — support for the mechanism, with a caveat.** "when memories are injected into the prompt, 10–25% are subtly altered" locates the alteration at injection, and her own previous line survives in the ~12-exchange history (`PromptBuilder.gd:434-440`), so the false version is still in front of the model on the turn the player challenges it. The mechanism is therefore not a contradiction. The residual risk is that "She will defend the false version" is not engineered: the same turn's `[memories]` layer carries the re-rolled true fact (measured: `Hana` on turn 2, true `Sarah` on turn 3), so the defence rests entirely on the model preferring its own history line over its memory block. I do not reject the reading; I recommend (a) recording it as a known limitation and (b) letting P2.22's playtests judge it before anyone adds prompt weight for it.
3. **Eviction tie-break is age — support for the tie-break, but see DES-3.** "oldest low-importance" as (lowest importance, then oldest) is a defensible reading and the test pins both halves. What it does not license is evicting the entry just accepted: that entry is the newest, and reporting it as `accepted` while discarding it is a contract error.
4. **Rule 3's content-word Jaccard — undisclosed reading, must be filed (DES-5).** Unlike 1–3 this one is not in the handoff's Deviations list, and the 0.667-vs-0.5 example shows it changes an outcome.
