### Deviations from the design bible

docs/01 §4's formulas are implemented unchanged; the items below are clarifications where the doc is silent
or where the implementation had to choose. None changes a formula or a threshold.

- **The three formula-only counters.** docs/01 §4's scores read `broken_promises`, `flattery_rejected` and
  `ignored_questions`, which docs/01 §2's tag list does not enumerate. They are stored in the same profile
  beside the twelve tags; `Paths.counters()` is the single list and `bump_profile`/`from_dict` accept it.
  Sources: `broken_promises` is engine-fed (docs/01 §5 I15 — the trigger engine bumps it; not wired yet,
  P2.10), `flattery_rejected` needs her reply's rejection marker, `ignored_questions` reads her previous
  direct question (docs/01 §5 T15 remains the model-detected authority; the keyword rule is a pre-emptive
  dismissive-reply bump). No DCR: the formulas and their inputs are the bible's; only the detection layer
  was unspecified.
- **Tie beyond recency.** §4 says "ties → the path with more recent evidence" and stops. If score *and*
  evidence tie (e.g. two counters bumped on the same turn), the path earlier in `paths` wins — the canonical
  docs order, and the same order the score report uses. Deterministic, data-owned (`paths.json`), tested.
- **Runner-up needs evidence.** §4's hybrid says the runner-up contributes 25% of its module; a zero-score
  path has no player evidence, so it is never a runner-up (otherwise every prompt would borrow flavour the
  player never earned). Positive-score requirement + "never the live primary" are both tested.
- **The lock fires at the crossing write, not only at a turn close.** `_refresh_stage` evaluates when a
  crossing lands at Stage ≥ 2, so the night roll-over (P2.19) and a trigger spike lock at the moment Stage 3
  is entered. That turn's own `read` lands after the lock; the path frozen is the one the crossing state
  produced. (The per-turn close still re-evaluates, so Stage-2 drift is turn-accurate.)
- **`apply_read` renamed `apply_turn_profile`.** It now owns read + keyword rules + the path check; the
  public-method count stays at gdlint's 20 (nothing was added to the class's public surface), and every call
  site is migrated. The keyword classifier is a separate class because `GameState` is already 887 lines of
  a 1000-line cap.
- **`path_runner_up` is computed after a lock.** Scores stay live (the overlay shows the truth); the frozen
  path does not change, and the runner-up follows the best *other* path. This keeps the hybrid alive across
  a locked run.

## Round 2  (2026-11-04, agent: deepseek)

### Summary

Round 2 fixes the keyword half of the behaviour profile — the two S2s (code CODE-1, design DES-1) and the
two S3s (CODE-2, CODE-3). The six formulas, the Stage-2 assign / Stage-3 lock, the recency tie-break and the
runner-up are untouched; all four acceptance criteria still hold and both scripted sets still land on their
documented paths (YANDERE 10.0, AWAKE 9.0).

The round-1 keyword rules read a *word* out of a message and scored the message as dismissive, so a warm
player could be assigned — and at Stage 3 permanently locked — HOLLOW for asking back, reassuring her or
answering her briefly. The data file's own contract was "a dismissive phrase, or a short reply", i.e. the
*message* is the behaviour. Round 2 makes the code obey that contract in three narrowings, all in
`_is_apathy()`:

1. **A question is never a dismissal.** The `?` check now runs first, before the phrase branch, instead of
   only on the short-reply branch.
2. **A dismissal phrase counts only when the line is short enough to *be* the dismissal.** New data cap
   `dismissal_max_words: 6`. "I don't care." (3 words) still fires; "I don't care about her, I care about
   you." (9 words) does not. A phrase match still wins over the exemption inside that cap.
3. **A terse reply to her pending direct question is an answer, not a dismissal.** `classify()` passes
   `awaiting_answer` (her previous direct question, docs/01 §5 T15), and the short-reply branch returns false
   while it is set. A long dismissal phrase still fires with a question pending ("Whatever." → apathy +
   ignored_questions), so T15 keeps its input.

Plus the two data/predicate fixes the reviews named: exemptions now match whole words through `_has_any()`
(so "I'm sorry" is exempt and "nothing"/"him" are not), and the generic phrases are gone — `control` lost
`you will` / `you must` / `i said`, `apathy` lost the ten ambiguous one-word stems (`bored`, `sure`, `fine`,
`cool`, `ok`, `okay`, `k`, `mhm`, `hm`, `lol`) that the short-reply cap already covers when they stand
alone.

### Deviations from the design bible

Round 1's six deviations stand (the design review supported all of them). One new, from this round:

- **DCR — a terse answer to her direct question is not a §4.4 "short reply".** docs/01 §4.4 lists "short
  replies" as a HOLLOW trigger and docs/01 §5 T15 counts ignoring her direct question; scoring a *two-word
  answer* to her question would count the same turn as both, and the design review's DES-1 regression
  requires such a line to leave apathy at zero. The implementation reads §4.4's "short replies" as replies
  that dismiss, and lets T15 own the "she asked, you brushed her off" case through the dismissal phrases
  (which still fire with a question pending). Impact: a player answering "At work" scores nothing; a player
  answering "Whatever." scores apathy + ignored_questions; a player replying "At work" to nothing scores
  apathy (short reply). No formula, threshold or counter changed.
  Proposed docs/01 §4.4 sentence (producer to schedule): *"A terse reply that answers her direct question is
  an answer, not a dismissal: it scores neither apathy nor T15's ignoring; a dismissal phrase with a
  question pending scores both."*
- **No docs edit was made** (the same policy as round 1): the DCR above is proposed here and the producer
  decides. The operational contract lives in `game/data/profile_keywords.json`'s `doc` string, which now
  states all three narrowings and the two word caps.
